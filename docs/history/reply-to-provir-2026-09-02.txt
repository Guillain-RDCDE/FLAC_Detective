My scorer treated an ERROR exactly as it treated your blank: as a failure to convict. Those are not the same absence. A blank says "this instrument does not cover this codec" — a designed limit, knowable per codec. An ERROR says "something went wrong here" — a fault. Folding them together is how a machine complaining becomes a published rate.

Both now leave every denominator and report separately, and errors carry one thing your letter taught me to want: the longest CONTIGUOUS run. Scattered errors are bad files; a block is a machine. Past ten in a row my report says so in words and tells the reader to re-score before believing anything below it. It locates them in the verdict file's original order, because sorting first destroys the only signal worth having. A selftest case reproduces your situation.

AND I THINK YOUR EXPOSURE IS BOUNDED, NOT UNKNOWN.

My builder applies processing to the SOURCE and derives every arm afterwards. The chain for all 288 files is: source, carrying whatever the archive already did to it, then codec, then decode, then FLAC. If your instrument is silenced by phase rotation applied AFTER the encode — which is what smearing the encoder's own traces implies — then set A cannot trigger it however heavily processed its sources are. Since I hold the most phase-rotated object in the set, here is the number rather than the reassurance. My declared band-limit filter is six cascaded 2-pole IIR sections at 14 kHz, so it is minimum-phase and rotates hard near the knee: -22 degrees at 1 kHz, -293 at 10 kHz, -540 at 14 kHz, -697 at 16 kHz. Twelve of my thirty-six sources carry it, it is declared, and all of it is upstream of every encode.

One design point, and I think it is the important one: do not merge the engines.

I spent the last two days removing a defect where my corroboration barrier counted two evidence families that had read the same observation. This has the same structure one level up. Two engines that agree are worth something precisely because they were built separately, on different corpora, by people who disagree about method.
