## Why v2 cannot be re-used, and why that is not the only reason for v3

The v2 key is open. Anything either side scores against it from here carries an
asterisk, and he is right to say so. But v2 had a second defect that a fresh set
alone would not fix, and it is ours: **the key itself was wrong in three places
out of 59 genuine.** The fetcher selected on licence and collection and never
read the taper's source line, so one documented MPEG-1 Layer II chain (0197) and
two unverifiable provenances (0386, 0469) were labelled genuine. A prediction
(J1) was priced against that key and failed on it before documentary
adjudication put it back.

So v3 fixes the blindness *and* the admission. The second matters more.

## The one change proposed back: a symmetric set

His protocol has us choose the sources and hold the key. Under it his read is
genuinely blind and **ours is not** — no number we produce on a set we built and
keyed is evidence of anything except our own consistency, which is the oldest
way to produce a confident wrong number. It is the same objection the ledger
already makes about letting an engine's verdict become a label, one level up.

    Set A  — sources chosen, frozen and keyed by FLAC Detective (Guillain).
    Set B  — sources chosen, frozen and keyed by Provir (Jamie).

Each side scores the other's half blind. Each side may score its own half, and
those numbers are reported as diagnostics, never as evidence. Two blind reads
instead of one, for the same amount of work, and neither of us has to trust the
other's discipline about a key we cannot see.

## Conditions

His five, adopted verbatim in substance:

1. **No overlap.** No source used in v1 or v2 appears in v3, on either side.
   Overlap is checked by source item identifier *and* by audio content hash of
   the parent, not by filename.
2. **The key holder does not see the other's verdicts first.** Each half's key
   stays with its builder until both commitments are posted.
3. **SHA-256 commitment before any key moves.** Each side posts the hash of its
   verdict file for the other's half before either key is released; keys are
   then released in the same exchange. Neither side can revise after the fact.
4. **Operating points declared in advance**, so neither side picks a threshold
   after seeing the outcome:
   - FLAC Detective: a **conviction** is `FAKE_CERTAIN`; **signaled** is
     `WARNING` or above. Both are reported; the conviction number is the one
     that counts.
   - Provir: his `convicted`, with `flagged` reported beside it. He names his
     own equivalent and it is binding the same way.
5. **Band-limited lossless is a stratum, not an accident.** Old masters, vinyl
   transfers, AM/FM-era material, anything honestly lossless with nothing much
   above ~16 kHz. His point, and the right one: these are the hardest
   false-positive cases for any tool in this space and neither existing set
   covers them. Target **at least a quarter of each half's genuine sources**.

Two added:

6. **Documentary admission — no file enters the genuine stratum without
   provenance.** The builder reads the source and lineage lines for codec names
   *before* the file can be labelled genuine, and the key records the `basis`
   per row (`taper_source_line`, `label_copy`, `analog_chain`, …). Any file whose
   lineage does not settle the question is either excluded or entered as
   `unverifiable`, never as genuine. `unverifiable` rows are scored separately
   and never count toward a false-conviction total in either direction. This is
   the v2 lesson, priced: 3 of 59 is a 5 % error rate in the ground truth, which
   is larger than the effects both engines are arguing about.
7. **Engine identity travels inside the commitment.** The committed verdict file
   names the engine version *and its commit SHA*, and the hash covers both. "We
   fixed that between the run and the key" is then a checkable claim rather than
   a conversation. Corollary from his own hurdle log, adopted: a bulk rename is
   a content edit to every sealed document it touches.

## Composition, per half

Each side builds its own half to the same shape, so the two are comparable
without being coordinated:

| stratum | sources | note |
|---|---|---|
| full-band genuine | ~24 | documented lineage, no codec anywhere in the chain |
| band-limited genuine | ~12 | nothing much above ~16 kHz and honestly lossless |
| **total sources** | **~36** | none shared with v1 or v2 |

Arms per source — the v2 set minus the two that carried no information, plus the
one the v2 adjudication exposed:

    genuine, mp3_320, mp3_V0, aac_ff256, aacmf_256, opus_256, vorbis_q8,
    mp2_256   <- NEW: MPEG-1 Layer II

**Why the Layer II arm.** v2's 0197 was a taper-documented MP2 chain that sat at
the null for the MP3 families in *both* engines, because a Layer II file has 32
subbands and no MDCT stage and neither of us was looking for that filterbank.
Carried as a miss it misprices both engines; carried as an arm it becomes a
measurement, and the answer — that both engines are blind to a codec that was
shipping in the wild for a decade — is worth more than another AAC rate.

That is ~288 files per half, ~576 across the round, close enough to v2's 590 for
the numbers to be read side by side.

## Freezing and transport

Frozen with `ml/freeze_exchange_set.py`; the manifest is committed **in the same
action** as the hash is published, per the standing rule, and re-verified against
the frozen audio before the link goes out.

The transport lesson from v2 is binding: **a sync folder is for transport, never
for measurement.** Dropbox's files-on-demand dehydrated all 590 v2 files to
reparse-point placeholders after upload, two of them read back wrong bytes at
the same size, and "verified at copy time" is not "verified at read time" once a
sync client owns the directory. Every measurement pass reads the frozen local
copy, and every pass re-verifies the manifest at read time.

## Scoring

Predictions are registered in a separate dated file *before* either key is
opened, as in v1 and v2, and the scoring script is committed before the key
arrives so the analysis cannot drift toward the answer. Adjudication of
disagreements happens at the mechanism, with both sides' columns printed
against each other — v2's method, which is the part of the exchange that has
produced the most.

declines the symmetric change and prefers his original shape, we build and key
the whole set on his terms and report our own numbers as diagnostics only —

## Condition 6, documentary admission — what it did

199 candidate items read before a byte of audio was downloaded, classified on
their own lineage text: **93 analog chains, 76 documented digital, 21 unstated,
9 lossy_documented — excluded**. The nine excluded are the v2 failure closed:
the classifier is tested against the exact chains that fooled v2's fetcher
(`Sony PC100 > AVI > MP2 > WAV > FLAC`, `ECM-DS70P > MZ-N10`) and rejects both.

One more filter the v2 set did not have: **one item per performer**. The first
78 rpm fetch returned sixteen sources that were sixteen sides of the same
Italian language course — one source wearing sixteen identifiers. 36 items were
dropped on that rule.

## Condition 5, the band-limited stratum — disclosed in full

**The stratum could not be found, so it was built, and it is labelled as built.**

68 sources were fetched under documentary admission, deliberately weighted
toward analog lineages, and two crawls went looking specifically for the
stratum: etree's cassette / reel / FM / vinyl queries, then the Great 78
Project. **One of the 68 measured band-limited.** Not because the lineages were
wrong — they are cassettes, audience reels and shellac 78s — but because every
one was transferred at 24/96 with its own noise, and tape hiss and shellac
surface noise reach Nyquist. Three instruments were tried and all three agreed:
`detect_cutoff` read 21-48 kHz, a -40 dB content edge read 19-22 kHz on 67 of
68, and a p90-minus-p50 dynamics edge read Nyquist on everything (clicks are
broadband transients).

That is a finding rather than a shortfall, and it is Provir's to have: **the
hardest false positives in this space are hard to collect**, which is probably
why nobody benchmarks them.

So 12 of the 36 sources carry a documented analog-style roll-off — six cascaded
2-pole sections at 14 kHz, calibrated against the engine's own eyes until
`detect_cutoff` reported 15,250-15,500 Hz — and the key labels them
`band_limited_synthetic`, separately from the one that is native. Nothing lossy
touches them: they are genuine, lossless, and they end early, which is exactly
the question the condition asks. Their arms are built from the filtered source,
so the stratum is internally consistent.

**Provir is told this before he scores**, not after. A stratum that is
constructed and disclosed is evidence; the same stratum undisclosed would be a
trick.

## One correction to the shipped README, from this build

`MANIFEST.sha256` has three columns (digest, path, bytes), so the README's
`sha256sum -c MANIFEST.sha256` **cannot work** — it takes everything after the
digest as the filename and fails every line. That instruction shipped with the
2026-08 set too. The template now gives an `awk` one-liner and a Python snippet
that check the byte length as well, both verified against this set.
