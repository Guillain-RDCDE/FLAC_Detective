1. THE HEADER CHUNK HAS A ONE-WORD FIX, AND IT IS A FLAG

He is adding a test that fails if any shipped file carries a LIST chunk. The
thing that puts it there is ffmpeg stamping its own muxer name, and it survives
`-map_metadata -1`, which is why it looked like it had been stripped.

Measured here: a FLAC written with `-map_metadata -1` alone still reports
`TAG:encoder=Lavf62.12.100` to ffprobe. The same command with `-bitexact` added
reports nothing at all. Our freezer has carried `-bitexact` since the v2 set,
which is the only reason set A r2 is clean — checked on the shipped files just
now, all three sampled report an empty tag set.

And the check we repaired this afternoon reads both format AND stream tags. Run
against his set B: `has_tags` returns True on b001.wav. **Our freezer would have
refused to ship his set**, which is the outcome he wants his new test to produce.
So the pair is: `-bitexact` at write time, and a tag check that reads stream tags
as well as format tags at freeze time.

2. FREE FORMAT: HIS CONSTRAINT IS EXACT, WITH THE NUMBERS

Checked rather than believed, because it decides how set C is built:

  10 s of 44.1 kHz stereo, LAME 3.100 `--freeformat -b 512` -> 643,656 bytes
  (~515 kbps).
  `ffmpeg -i free512.mp3 out.wav` -> `[mp3float] Header missing`, repeated 199
  times, no audio produced. Exit is not the problem; the silence is.
  `lame --decode free512.mp3 out.wav` -> 1,764,044 bytes, correct.

Our own arm builder encodes through ffmpeg's libmp3lame, which can neither write
nor read free format, so if we ever build that arm it has to shell out to LAME
too. `ml/free_format.py` now carries the decode path, and it hashes the decoder
binary before executing it — the rule we already applied to his encoder
collection. Sending the decoder build with its hash is the right call, and a week
early is better than on the day.

3. HIS EXCERPT PROBLEM, ASKED OF OUR OWN ENGINE

His first confession — a number priced on one excerpt, quoted as if it belonged
to the file — is the kind that transposes, so it was asked here rather than
assumed away.

What the audit found, before any measurement: our accusing rules do not share the
defect in the same shape. Rules 11, 13, 14 and 15 read the WHOLE file, not an
excerpt. Rule 10 compares two segments and escalates to five when they disagree.
The spectral path samples three positions — start, middle, end. And rule 12, the
CNN, used to infer on a single 10-second window from the middle of the file and
was repaired for exactly his reason: the comment in the source says a quiet
intro or a band-limited bridge made the verdict hostage to one segment. It now
samples three evenly spaced windows and surfaces their spread.

So the excerpt length only reaches the spectral and quality paths. That is not
the same as knowing our verdicts are stable, which nobody here had measured
either, so it was measured: forty files — two band-limited sources and three
full-band ones, all eight arms of each — scored at 15, 30 and 60 seconds.

**Zero verdicts moved.** Not one conviction gained or lost, not one signalling
change, at either comparison against the 30 s baseline.

That number came with a warning attached, and the warning is worth more than the
result. The 15 s and 60 s verdict files came out byte-identical, hash and all,
which is exactly what a knob that does nothing produces. So the knob was given a
positive control before the stability was believed: calling the spectral analysis
directly at the three lengths on five files shows the measured energy ratio does
move — 0.003147, 0.002950, 0.002933 on one of them — while the detected cutoff
does not move by a single hertz. The parameter is live. What is stable is the
cutoff, because an encoder's wall is a property of the file rather than of the
window you look at it through, and the cutoff is what the scoring rules read.

One limit was stated as soon as that number existed: set A is 60-second excerpts,
so it varies the window inside an excerpt and never reaches the regime where the
spectral path takes THREE positions instead of one — which is where a real file
sits, and where a user actually is. That regime has since been measured too.

Twelve full vinyl-transfer tracks, 90 to 433 seconds, at the same three lengths:
nothing moved. But all twelve read AUTHENTIC, and a verdict that nothing is
pulling on cannot move, so on its own that number is worth very little.

So the cases that are actually contested were built: those same full tracks
re-encoded at 320 kbps and at Vorbis q8, then back to FLAC. Eight files spanning
every tier the engine has — FAKE_CERTAIN, SUSPICIOUS, WARNING and AUTHENTIC, so
files sitting at thresholds rather than far from them. **Nothing moved there
either, and every score is identical to the point at all three lengths.**

Two of those eight are worth his attention for another reason: two full-length
320 kbps transcodes read AUTHENTIC. That is a miss, not an instability, and it is
the same weakness his half already showed — our mp3_320 conviction rate is 11 per
cent on our own material.

His defect and ours are therefore not the same shape, and the answer only means
anything because the knob was proved live first.


4. OUR OWN STRATUM MAP, MEASURED FROM THIS SIDE AT LAST

He verified our band-limited map against our audio because we asked him not to
take the file date for it. We had never verified it ourselves, which is elegant
once and a gap if it stays that way. So it was measured here, deliberately NOT
with the engine's own `detect_cutoff` — checking a map against the instrument
that drew it proves only that the instrument is consistent — but with his method
written from his description: a plain long-term average spectrum, reference in
10-13 kHz, first frequency falling 20 dB below it.

All 96 band-limited files measurable, all 96 inside 14,250 to 14,621 Hz, median
14,516. His figures were 14,174 to 14,573, median 14,486 — the offset is method
(Welch window against his LTAS), the agreement is total. **Not one of the 96 sits
outside.** And the edge is in the same place on the `genuine` arm as on the seven
encoded ones, which is the check that says the filter puts it there rather than
the encoder.

On his ten full-band files falling below 16 kHz, we find eleven. His eight from
src030 reproduce exactly, across all eight of its classes including its genuine.
His two MP2 rows at "15.9 and 16.0 kHz" are src034 at 15,962 and src029 at
15,972. The eleventh is **src031's mp2_256 at 15,843 Hz** — lower than either of
the two he named, and absent from his list. One row, named, so he can look rather
than take our word for it.
