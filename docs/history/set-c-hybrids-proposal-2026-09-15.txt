Set C — what the answer key says about hybrids (proposal, 2026-09-15)

A hybrid is a lossless master that was never encoded as a whole, but carries
one or more lossy-sourced components: a drum hit from a sample pack shipped
as MP3, a vocal lifted from a video, a stem bounced through a codec before
the mix. The container is lossless, the mix was never transcoded, and yet a
band of the spectrum, or a few seconds of it, carries codec scars.

Why the key has to say something before anyone scores

Both engines will fire on some hybrids and stay silent on others. If the key
labels a hybrid "genuine", every hit is charged as a false positive for
something that really is lossy. If it labels it "lossy-sourced", every miss
is charged as a false negative for a file whose master was never encoded.
Either way the false-positive figure stops measuring what it claims to
measure, and the two engines are compared on a definition neither was built
for. The crash909 pack in the Squad-E drive, if it is in as many UK hardcore
releases as it looks, makes this the common case in one whole genre, not an
edge case.

What is proposed

1. Three labels in the Set C key, not two: GENUINE (no lossy material
   anywhere), TRANSCODED (the whole programme passed through a codec), and
   HYBRID (a lossless master containing lossy-sourced material). A verdict is
   still never a label; the key is what the builder knows about how the file
   was made.

2. A hybrid is scored on its own line. Neither a hit nor a miss on a HYBRID
   file counts toward the false-positive rate or the recall of the other two
   classes. The numbers we publish are precision and recall on GENUINE vs
   TRANSCODED; the HYBRID line is reported separately, as "how many of the N
   hybrids did each engine flag, and on what evidence".

3. Set C carries a small HYBRID arm on purpose, built rather than found:
   genuine masters with one known lossy sample mixed in at a known level and
   a known time, so the key can say where the lossy material is. Found
   hybrids (a commercial release suspected of carrying the pack) stay out of
   the denominator until the sample is matched in it by a method that does
   not depend on either engine — correlation against the known sample, not a
   separated stem. A neural separator leaves its own scars, and measuring
   codec traces in a Demucs output measures Demucs.

4. The builder of a HYBRID file declares what is lossy in it (the sample, its
   bitrate, the passages where it plays nearly alone). That is answer-key
   material, sealed with the rest before scoring, released with it after.

5. Nothing changes for the band-limited stratum, the free-format arms, the
   two AAC encoders or the low-bitrate arms; the hybrid arm is additional and
   small (a dozen files is enough for a first line, it is not a calibration
   set).

What it costs each side

Provir: the Heartbeatz question. If the pack is in it and Provir fires on it,
that file moves from the lawful control pool to the HYBRID line and the
current false-positive figure is recomputed without it. Reported either way.

FLAC Detective: the same. Our verdict on a hybrid is "lossy-sourced" today
and that word is wrong for it; a third word ("contains lossy material",
with the evidence pointing at the band or the passage) is engine work that
comes after the key exists, not before.

Sequence: agree the three labels, then Provir builds the stratum and the
hybrid arm, then both keys are sealed, then anyone scores.
