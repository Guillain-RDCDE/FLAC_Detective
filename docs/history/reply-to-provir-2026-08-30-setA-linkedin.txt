Now the part you need before you score, because it is the one place where set A does not do what the protocol says.

The band-limited stratum could not be found. Sixty-eight sources were fetched under documentary admission, deliberately weighted toward analog lineages, and then two crawls went looking specifically for the stratum: etree's cassette, reel, FM and vinyl queries, and then the Great 78 Project. One of the sixty-eight measured band-limited. Not because the lineages are wrong — they are cassettes, audience reels and shellac 78s — but because every one of them was transferred at 24/96 with its own noise, and tape hiss and shellac surface noise reach Nyquist. I tried three instruments and all three agreed: my own detect_cutoff read 21 to 48 kHz, a -40 dB content edge read 19 to 22 kHz on 67 of the 68, and a p90-minus-p50 dynamics edge read Nyquist on everything, because clicks are broadband transients. By a detector's eyes these files are not band-limited, so they are not the population your condition is about.

I think that is a finding rather than a shortfall, and it is yours as much as mine: the hardest false positives in this space are hard to collect, which is probably why nobody benchmarks them.

So twelve of the thirty-six sources carry a documented analog-style roll-off — six cascaded 2-pole sections at 14 kHz, chosen by calibrating against my own engine's eyes until detect_cutoff reported 15,250 to 15,500 Hz — and the key labels them band_limited_synthetic, separately from the one that is native. Nothing lossy touches them: they are genuine and lossless and they end early, which is the question your condition asks. Their arms are built from the filtered source, so the stratum is internally consistent. A constructed stratum disclosed before scoring is evidence; the same stratum undisclosed would be a trick, which is why this paragraph exists.

Separately, and while set A was building, I ran the two experiments your family lock suggested to me, both registered before they ran, and both were refused by their own bounds. I would rather send you the failures than the tidy version.

Counting generations. If the idem fixed point is a fixed point, distance to it should fall with each re-encode. It does, in the median: on a libmp3lame-320 ladder the median R reads 0.877, 0.486, 0.311, 0.227 across four generations, and no master falls below the one-generation median. But per file only 11 of 24 are strictly monotone, and gen1 against gen2 reads AUC 0.644 against the 0.75 I had registered. Post-hoc: gen1 vs gen3 is 0.807 and gen1 vs gen4 is 0.835. So counting reads at two generations apart and not at one, and I will not be claiming that the engine counts generations. One of my five criteria was also mis-specified rather than failed — I predicted the phase would drift past the first generation, and it does not, because a clean re-encode chain is delay-trimmed back to the start every time; drift comes from editing, which is your wild population and not my ladder. Withdrawn explicitly, kept in the document.

Encoder attribution, the bank of probes your family lock implies: one round-trip per family, the file attributed to whichever probe it falls furthest under. All five predictions failed, and the defect was mine — I compared raw R across five probes as though one scale fitted all of them, and the twolame probe simply sits lower than the others, so it won 22 of 60 lossy files and half the genuine ones. Centre each probe on its own median over genuine material and scale by its own spread, and the picture is sharp: mp3_320 12 of 12, opus_256 12 of 12, aac_ff256 2 of 12, vorbis_q8 2 of 12, aacmf_256 0 of 12. Self-pairing is real for MP3 and for Opus and absent for AAC and Vorbis — and not for want of an encoder match, since the AAC arm was made by the very encoder its probe uses, at the same bitrate, and still reads at chance.

The safety criterion failed too, and that one I am not working around: genuine masters are attributed confidently rather than abstaining, so the instrument invents provenance on a file that has none, and no number from it goes near a wild file in either direction until there is a calibrated null and an abstention rule with a real bar under it.

What I did not expect is that the same boundary showed up twice in one day from two directions. The generation ladder finds no convergence at all in ffmpeg's AAC — 3.27, 3.31, 3.35, 3.34, AUC 0.493, chance — and the attribution bank finds no self-pairing in it either. Some codecs have a re-encode fixed point that a second pass can find, and ffmpeg's AAC does not. That is the shape of a real question, and it is one your bench can answer from the other side, since your Fraunhofer arms behave differently from your LAME ones under exactly this test.
